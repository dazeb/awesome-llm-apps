# Show HN: Retracer – replay production requests against your staging branch before you deploy

On March 3rd we shipped a one-line change to a Go service that parses shipping addresses. Unit tests passed. Integration tests passed. Code review took four minutes. Eleven hours later, 4.2% of checkout requests in production were returning 422s, because a small fraction of real Brazilian addresses contain a district field our fixtures never had. It took us 6 hours to notice (the errors were spread across regions, so no single alert fired), 90 minutes to bisect, and one very uncomfortable postmortem to admit the obvious: our test data was a fantasy. Production traffic was the only dataset that mattered, and we had never once run our code against it before deploying.

Retracer is the tool we built after that incident. It sits as a sidecar next to your service, samples real production requests (headers, bodies, and the responses your current code returned), scrubs PII with configurable field-level rules, and stores them as replayable traces. When you open a PR, Retracer replays the last N thousand traces against a container built from your branch and diffs every response, status code, header, and body, field by field, against what production actually returned. The March bug shows up as a red line in your PR: "312 of 50,000 replayed requests changed status 200 → 422." You see it before merge, not eleven hours after deploy.

It works today with Go, Python, and Node services that speak HTTP or gRPC. Setup is a sidecar container and a 20-line config file. Replay runs take about 4 minutes for 50k traces on our hosted runners, or you can run them in your own CI.

Retracer empowers teams to ship with confidence, reduce risk, and eliminate the anxiety of the deploy button. By harnessing the power of real traffic, it transforms your release process from a leap of faith into a data-driven decision. Teams that adopt Retracer report dramatically improved velocity and a renewed sense of trust in their pipelines.

In their first month it flagged 3 PRs that passed CI but changed responses for real traffic, including one where a currency-rounding refactor altered prices by one cent on 0.8% of quotes. Retracer is open source (Apache 2.0) with a hosted option for teams that don't want to run the replay infrastructure themselves. The free tier covers 10k replayed traces per PR, which is enough for most single-service setups. You can go from install to your first replay diff in under 30 minutes with the quickstart.

Try it: `docker run retracer/init` in any repo with a Dockerfile, or read the docs at retracer.dev/quickstart. If you replay your traffic and it catches nothing, that's a good day. The first time it catches something, you'll wonder how you ever deployed without it. We'd love feedback, especially from teams with gRPC-heavy stacks, since that's where our diffing is newest.
